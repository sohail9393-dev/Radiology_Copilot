Pneumonia is an infection that inflames the air sacs (alveoli) in one or both lungs, causing them to fill with fluid or pus. On a chest X-ray, healthy lung tissue appears dark because air allows X-rays to pass through easily; infected areas appear as white or hazy "opacities" because fluid and inflammatory material block that passage. Radiologists distinguish between several patterns: lobar pneumonia typically shows a dense, well-defined opacity confined to a single lobe, often with visible air bronchograms (dark, air-filled airways silhouetted against the surrounding white consolidation); bronchopneumonia appears as patchy, scattered opacities across both lungs; and interstitial pneumonia (often viral) produces a finer, lace-like reticular pattern. Chest X-rays are also used alongside clinical criteria (such as fever, tachycardia, or abnormal breath sounds) to decide whether imaging is warranted in the first place, and a normal-looking X-ray does not fully rule out early or mild pneumonia.
