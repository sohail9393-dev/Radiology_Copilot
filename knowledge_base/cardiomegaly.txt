Cardiomegaly refers to enlargement of the cardiac silhouette on chest imaging. On a PA (posteroanterior) chest X-ray, it is most reliably assessed using the cardiothoracic ratio (CTR) -- the width of the heart divided by the width of the thoracic cavity at its widest point -- with a ratio greater than 0.5 generally considered abnormal. Common causes include congestive heart failure, hypertension with left ventricular hypertrophy, valvular disease, and various cardiomyopathies. The CTR should specifically be measured on a PA view rather than an AP (portable/bedside) view, since AP films tend to magnify the heart's apparent size due to how the X-ray beam diverges, which can lead to falsely diagnosing cardiomegaly on a portable film. In equivocal cases, echocardiography is used to confirm true chamber enlargement and to distinguish it from other causes of an enlarged cardiac silhouette, such as a pericardial effusion.
