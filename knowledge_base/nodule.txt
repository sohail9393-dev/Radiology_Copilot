A pulmonary nodule is a small, round or oval-shaped opacity in the lung, generally defined as being between roughly 7 and 30 millimeters in diameter; smaller lesions are typically termed micronodules, while larger ones (over 30mm) are classified as a pulmonary mass instead. On X-ray, nodules typically appear well-defined, with sharp margins, and are surrounded by otherwise normal-appearing lung tissue. They are frequently discovered incidentally on imaging performed for unrelated reasons, and in most cases -- particularly in younger patients -- they turn out to be benign, often the result of an old, healed infection or scarring. However, if a patient has risk factors such as a smoking history, or if the nodule increases in size on follow-up imaging, further workup (such as a CT scan) is generally recommended to rule out malignancy.
